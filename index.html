🏙️ 1. Network Topology & Cell Architecture

For a mid-sized city spanning a 5.0 km radius, the service area is modeled using a regular hexagonal grid pattern. Hexagons provide seamless coverage without overlapping or coverage gaps. To handle non-uniform traffic density across the city, hierarchical cell sizing is deployed:

Dense Urban Core

Cell Radius ($R$) = 500 m[cite: 32]. Maximizes frequency reuse and subscriber capacity in high-rise commercial zones.

Suburban Residential

Cell Radius ($R$) = 1.5 km. Optimized for medium subscriber density and wider coverage footprints.

Open / Highway Area

Cell Radius ($R$) = 3.0 km. Extended range coverage with minimal terrain obstruction.

📡 4. Base Station Antenna Configuration

To optimize spatial coverage and suppress inter-cell interference, a 3-sector antenna configuration is deployed per base station site[cite: 32].

Antenna Gain & Beamwidth

Directional panel antennas with 18 dBi gain and 65° horizontal half-power beamwidth (HPBW) per sector.

Electrical & Mechanical Tilt

Configured with 6° electrical down-tilt and 2° mechanical down-tilt to confine cell footprint and prevent overshoot interference.

Sector Azimuth Layout:

• Sector A: 000° (North)

• Sector B: 120° (Southeast)

• Sector C: 240° (Southwest)

🔄 5. Handoff Strategy & Interference Mitigation

Handoff Algorithm

Implements a Relative Signal Strength with Hysteresis and Threshold handoff mechanism. A mobile station initiates handoff to a target base station only if:

RSSI_target > RSSI_serving + Hysteresis (3 dB)

This prevents "ping-pong" handoffs at cell boundaries.

Interference Mitigation

• Co-Channel Interference (CCI): Minimized by maintaining strict cluster reuse distance ($Q = 4.58$)[cite: 31].
• Adjacent Channel Interference (ACI): Controlled using sharp crystal bandpass filters and guard bands.
• Power Control: Uplink/downlink adaptive power control reduces near-far effect and overall network noise floor.
